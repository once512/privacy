Privacy Policy for KeyDog - File Encryption

Last updated: November 9, 2026

KeyDog (密钥狗), application ID app.keydog, is a file encryption application provided by KongQBin (GitHub account: once512). This policy covers version 1.2.2 (23). This is the initial production release, with advertising and optional paid Pro access.

1. Local processing and data collection

KeyDog reads files and folders you select to encrypt or restore their contents. File contents, names, directory structure, sizes and integrity hashes are processed on your device to perform these operations and check the results. Encryption, decryption, password derivation and preset-passphrase recovery run locally.

We do not upload selected file contents, passwords or keys to an app backend or include them in ad requests. KeyDog does not require its own account or operate its own payment or file server. Google advertising, consent and billing services process additional data as described below; local cryptographic processing does not mean the entire app is network-free.

2. File access and permissions

KeyDog uses Android's system document picker (Storage Access Framework). You grant access to the files or folders you choose; folder access can include the files within that folder. The app may retain the system's document-access grant so it can finish or resume an operation and access that location later, subject to Android and the document provider's permissions.

Internet and network-state access support Google advertising, privacy choices and Google Play Billing. Google's SDKs may declare advertising-ID and advertising-service permissions, and Billing declares its payment permission. The app does not request all-files access (MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE), broad storage, notification, microphone, camera, contacts or runtime location permissions. Advertising services may estimate approximate location from an IP address without a location permission.

The system picker may offer a cloud storage provider. If you choose one, that provider may download, upload or synchronize selected files, outputs and deletions under its own settings and privacy policy. Local cryptographic processing does not prevent transfers performed by a document provider.

3. Data stored on your device

  • Output files and metadata: Encrypted containers and restored files are written to the selected destination. Containers carry original-file metadata for restoration and integrity checks. Encrypted filenames retain the original name followed by .kdog. Key filenames also include the original name, so filenames can reveal information even while file contents are encrypted.
  • Dog protection: A “dog” in the app is a cryptographic key-share file, not an animal or an online account. Generated .dog key files are stored in the app's private storage until you release (delete) them or the app removes corresponding keys during undo or successful recovery. Android's app sandbox restricts access by other ordinary apps; key files are not separately protected by your encryption password. Keep exported keys secure and store threshold shares separately when appropriate.
  • Passwords and preset passphrases: Password input is used for the requested operation; ordinary password input is not written to a password database. To let you retrieve the full preset passphrase after an interruption, the app stores it encrypted in its pending-operation record, protected by a key held in Android Keystore. This record remains until you resolve the pending result or undo the operation.
  • Temporary files: File processing and verification can create temporary archives, including plaintext copies, in the app's private cache. The app attempts to remove these when processing ends and removes leftover KeyDog cache files at startup. An interruption may leave temporary files until cleanup occurs.
  • Operation records and preferences: Pending-operation records can include document locations, relative paths, sizes, hashes, created outputs and key references. These support integrity verification, undo and safe handling of interrupted operations. Records are removed when the operation is resolved. Preferences include the chosen app language, the preset-passphrase warning setting, separate encryption and recovery Dog-warning preferences, successful daily encryption/recovery counts, cached store-ownership status, last verification times and separate earned-ad encryption and recovery credit balances and the last weekly reset. These are kept locally until changed or app data is cleared. The app does not send its usage-counter records or encryption results to a developer-operated server. On Android 13 and later the system also stores the app-language selection.

Android automatic app backup is disabled in the current app. Files you save or export outside private app storage may still be backed up or synchronized by your device or document provider.

4. Export, sharing and the clipboard

When you export keys, copies are written to the destination you choose. When you share keys, Android grants the receiving app temporary read access to the selected files. That app or service controls how it subsequently stores or transfers them. We cannot remove copies already exported, shared or backed up.

On the encryption screen, “Manage my dogs” offers export, sharing and release of app-stored Dog files. Export and direct in-app sharing, including batch sharing, are available without Pro. These file-management actions do not consume encryption or recovery uses. On the recovery screen, “Use dogs in this app” only selects internal Dog files for recovery; it does not offer export, sharing or release. External Dog files can be selected through the system document picker. Selecting a file does not itself copy it into private key storage.

If you choose to copy a full preset passphrase, it is placed on the Android clipboard. Access and retention depend on Android and your device's clipboard features, including any enabled synchronization. Treat copied passphrases as sensitive and clear them when no longer needed.

5. Retention, deletion and recovery

After encryption and verification, you can keep the original files, request their deletion, or undo encryption. Undo attempts to delete this operation's generated encrypted files and app-stored keys while retaining the originals. After a successful restore and verification, the app attempts to delete the encrypted input and corresponding keys, including selected external keys where deletion is permitted. Missing provider permissions or an interrupted operation can prevent cleanup; check any reported cleanup issue.

In “Manage my dogs”, “Release” means deleting the selected app-stored Dog files after confirmation. The confirmation asks you to ensure you have a safe backup. It does not transfer them to another person, revoke exported copies or make them recoverable later. Clearing app data or uninstalling removes private app data, including stored keys and pending records. Outputs, exported keys, shared copies, and cloud-provider copies outside private app storage are not removed by uninstalling. Manage those through their respective storage provider or receiving service. Clearing app data or uninstalling can also remove local daily counts, cached ownership and earned-ad credits. Valid paid purchases can be restored through the same Google Play account; earned-ad credits are local and are not account-backed purchases.

Deletion is ordinary file deletion and does not guarantee secure erasure of flash storage, provider trash, backups or synchronized copies. Before deleting keys or clearing app data, make sure you have the password or enough independently saved key shares needed to recover your files.

Threshold recovery needs at least the configured number of distinct valid shares from the same encryption operation. Copying or renaming one Dog file does not create an additional share, and shares for another operation cannot replace missing ones. Private app storage is not an independent backup: clearing data, uninstalling, device loss or device failure can make stored Dog files unavailable.

We do not hold a server-side recovery copy of your passwords or keys. If the required secret or enough key shares are lost, we cannot recover them for you. Preset-passphrase mode deliberately permits local candidate searching: with an empty original password, another person using the same public presets and rules can also recover the file. A short original password can also be guessed. This mode does not provide the same protection as a strong private password or securely held keys.

6. Google advertising, purchases and support

Advertising: Non-Pro users may see native ads below the encryption or recovery action. Users can voluntarily complete a rewarded ad to earn one encryption use from the encryption screen, or one recovery use from the recovery screen. Credits are granted only when the SDK confirms the reward and are tracked separately. Daily free uses are consumed before ad credits; only successful tasks count. Unused ad credits reset every Monday at the start of the device’s local calendar day. Ad credits do not activate Pro or hide native ads. Direct key sharing is available to free users without ad credits. Active Pro hides native ads. Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob, version 25.5.0 in this release) processes and shares network and device information, including IP address, advertising/app-set identifiers and applicable device/account identifiers, ad and app interactions, and performance/diagnostic information. These support ad delivery, measurement, analytics and fraud prevention. IP addresses may be used to infer approximate location. The SDK protects its transmitted data with TLS.

Your choices: Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) requests applicable privacy choices before ad requests are allowed. A “Privacy choices” entry in the Pro dialog is shown when required by UMP. You can also use Android's advertising-ID controls and Google's ad settings. Ad personalization and available ad types depend on region, settings and consent. Declining an optional advertising choice does not remove the daily free file-processing allowance. Pro hides native placements but does not disable necessary billing or privacy-service communication; rewarded ads remain voluntary where offered.

Purchases: Monthly Pro is an automatically renewing subscription; permanent Pro is a one-time purchase for KeyDog. Purchases are handled by Google Play Billing. Buying permanent Pro does not cancel an existing monthly subscription; manage or cancel its renewal separately in Google Play subscriptions. Uninstalling does not cancel a subscription. Google processes payment/account information under its own terms. The app receives product, purchase-token, transaction-state and ownership information to activate access, acknowledge purchases and restore ownership through Google Play. We do not receive your card number or billing address. Subscription ownership is periodically refreshed, with a bounded local offline cache; the app does not receive an exact subscription-expiration date through its Billing client. Pending payments do not activate Pro until confirmed as purchased.

Relevant third-party information: Google Privacy Policy, Google Mobile Ads data disclosure and Google ad controls. Third-party retention and deletion are governed by their policies and your account controls. We cannot delete Google-held billing or advertising records from local app storage.

Support: If you email us, we receive the address, message and attachments you choose to send. We use them to respond and handle the request, retaining correspondence only as needed for that purpose or legal requirements. Our email provider processes it to deliver and store email. Do not send passwords, private keys or sensitive file contents. You can request deletion of support correspondence at the address below, subject to required retention.
